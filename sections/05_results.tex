\subsection{Controlled impairments}

Figure~\ref{fig:impairments} and Table~\ref{tab:quantitative-results} report the main impairment results. The figure is a dissertation-side composite regenerated from retained evidence; the project-generated delay and loss audit SVGs were checked but omit the replicated bandwidth cohorts and interval information. The direct historical 20-Mbps record measured 19.2 Mbps, a $-4.00\%$ deviation, but supplies no interval. The supplementary 50-Mbps cohort measured 41.500 Mbps (standard deviation (SD) of 3.362, 95\% confidence interval (CI) $\pm4.175$, range 36.9--45.8), a $-17.00\%$ deviation. At 100 Mbps the five valid measurements averaged 78.780 Mbps (SD 11.684, 95\% CI $\pm14.508$, range 58.4--87.3), a $-21.22\%$ deviation. These 50/100-Mbps statistics are repository-reported from the retained supplementary cohort. The dual-router 20-Mbps benchmark is excluded from this direct-topology panel.

Mean RTT increased from 0.105 ms at 0-ms configured one-way delay to 27.008, 80.303, and 133.686 ms at 10, 30, and 50 ms. The corresponding AC sample standard deviations (SDs) were 0.019, 0.056, 0.057, and 0.037 ms. The overall permutation Kruskal--Wallis test gave $H=17.857$, permutation $p<10^{-4}$, and $\epsilon^2=0.929$. This is an overall association, not pairwise evidence. These observations are RTTs, not measured one-way latency.

Configured one-way loss of 0, 1, 3, and 5\% produced mean end-to-end ping loss of 0, 3, 6, and 10\%. The model-based round-trip references $100[1-(1-p/100)^2]$ are 0, 1.99, 5.91, and 9.75\%; they are not an experimental baseline. Across five runs per condition, the observed SDs were 0, 2.345, 3.240, and 3.391 percentage points. The overall permutation test gave $H=15.611$, permutation $p<10^{-4}$, and $\epsilon^2=0.788$. No corrected pairwise test was performed. Aggregate received/sent counts were 500/500, 485/500, 470/500, and 450/500.

The separate dual-router benchmark completed five valid runs with a mean throughput of 19.220 Mbps, SD 0.045, 95\% CI $\pm0.056$, and range 19.2--19.3 Mbps. The mean is RR; dispersion and range are AC.

\subsection{Topology scaling and Germany50}

Figure~\ref{fig:topology-scaling} separates simple execution, the dual-router benchmark, and full Germany50 scope. The one-, four-, and nine-hop cases produced mean RTTs of 41.196, 104.623, and 214.425 ms and throughputs of 18.8, 17.8, and 15.7 Mbps. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:appendix-germany50} gives path detail and dispersion. Hop count and cumulative configured per-link delay co-vary, so these descriptive cases do not isolate a topology-only effect.

Selected mode installed 20 routes and completed three ping/iperf3 measurements for each representative pair (nine total). The full 4,224-entry plan separately returned dry-run success. No retained evidence shows that all entries were installed or that all node pairs exchanged real traffic.

\subsection{AI, RL-based closed-loop control, and Dashboard}

Table~\ref{tab:extended-validation} summarizes the extensions. The official OpenAI attempt returned Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP) 429 with \texttt{insufficient\_quota}; it generated no successful official scenario. The separate Qwen-compatible request produced a validated scenario and a single controlled real-Docker result of 82.194-ms RTT, 18.3-Mbps throughput, and 0\% measured loss. No SD or CI is available for this single validation.

\input{tables/table_3_extended_validation.tex}

All four policies reused the same Docker execution, route-verification, measurement, and evidence pipeline. After initialization, only Q-learning used prior measured RTT, loss, throughput, and current-path state to update a Q-table; the heuristic selected from the known phase configuration, while fixed A/B remained fixed. The threshold heuristic achieved RR mean reward 17.116 (RR SD 0.325 and RR 95\% interval half-width $\pm0.142$), mean RTT 30.189 ms, 0\% mean loss, and 18.625-Mbps mean throughput. Q-learning achieved RR mean reward 4.143 (RR SD 15.045 and RR interval half-width $\pm6.594$), mean RTT 89.225 ms, 2.5\% loss, and 9.855-Mbps throughput. These summaries are descriptive. Q-learning selected path A in all 20 recorded rows and showed no path switch after the phase boundary; the heuristic selected A for ten rows, B for ten, and switched once. The Q-learning rows span a migration and agent reinitialization, so they are not an uninterrupted learning trajectory. These single-seed ordered observations are not independent randomized replications and do not support RL superiority. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:appendix-rl} gives the full summary.
