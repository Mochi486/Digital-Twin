\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{RQ1: controllability and repeatability}

\paragraph{Answer.} The workflow enforced configured conditions that were associated with measured bandwidth, RTT, and loss changes, without making configured and measured quantities equivalent.

\paragraph{Evidence.} Delay and loss each showed an overall association in permutation tests, with $\epsilon^2=0.929$ and 0.788 respectively. Loss means were 0, 3, 6, and 10\% against model references of 0, 1.99, 5.91, and 9.75\%, but $n=5$ gives wide uncertainty and no pairwise inference. In the balanced cohort, mean throughput was 19.20, 47.80, and 94.76 Mbps at configured 20, 50, and 100 Mbps, with five valid runs per rate.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} TBF sets a token rate, whereas iperf3 observes end-to-end TCP throughput. Likewise, qdisc delay and loss are one-way interface settings, whereas ping reports a bidirectional path. Protocol behaviour, queue placement, scheduling, and shared-host load explain why enforcement and observation must remain separate constructs.

\paragraph{Relation to literature.} This result follows the \texttt{netem} warning that timers, queues, and protocol behaviour affect observations \citep{iproute2netem}. It also gives an empirical instance of the fidelity--efficiency trade-off identified for NDT models \citep{hui2023digitaltwin}: a lightweight twin exposes controlled trends without establishing physical-network fidelity.

\paragraph{Limitation.} Most cohorts contain five runs, and packet-loss intervals are wide. Bandwidth ran in fixed 20/50/100-Mbps blocks within one session, so rate co-varies with execution order and time. The evidence supports the tested ranges on one Docker/WSL host only.

\subsection{RQ2: topology extension and routing}

\paragraph{Answer.} The validated pipeline extends from a small routed case to two routers and to Germany50 structural scale, while large-topology real traffic remains representative rather than exhaustive.

\paragraph{Evidence.} Five dual-router runs carried near-20-Mbps traffic. The extracted one-, four-, and nine-hop paths each carried three measured flows. The full 50-node/88-link graph was instantiated; selected mode installed 20 routes for three endpoint pairs and produced nine measurements, while the 4,224-entry full plan passed dry-run only.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The small topology tests execution, the dual-router case tests explicit forwarding, and Germany50 tests graph construction, route planning, and selected-path operation. In the selected paths, hop count and cumulative configured delay co-vary, so RTT and throughput differences cannot be attributed to hop count alone. Dry-run validates structure and command planning, not runtime installation or all-pairs correctness.

\paragraph{Relation to literature.} Topology Zoo contributes realistic structural inputs rather than execution evidence \citep{knight2011topologyzoo}. The results connect that structural resource to a container experiment while retaining the distinction, central to reproducible emulation \citep{handigol2012reproducible}, between a runnable specification and the traffic actually observed.

\paragraph{Limitation.} Only three Germany50 endpoint pairs carried real traffic, and path factors are confounded. The study therefore makes no all-pairs, wide-area, or operational-scale claim.

\subsection{RQ3: AI and RL-based closed-loop extensibility}

\paragraph{Answer.} Guarded generation and measured closed-loop control can reuse the workflow, but provider availability and controller performance limit the result. RL serves only as a bounded closed-loop path-selection use case: it tests whether measured RTT, loss, and throughput can feed a controller through the shared execution-and-evidence pipeline. The contribution lies in integrating and evaluating RL-based closed-loop control within the shared pipeline, rather than proposing a new RL algorithm or claiming superiority over the heuristic.

\paragraph{Evidence.} The Qwen-compatible scenario passed validation and one controlled execution; the official OpenAI request returned HTTP 429. The retained comparison contains 80 valid Docker measurement rows across four policies. The threshold heuristic switched at the phase boundary and had a higher descriptive mean reward. Q-learning selected path A in all 20 recorded Q-learning rows and showed no path switch after the boundary.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Projection and validation constrain generated text before execution, so the observation demonstrates a guarded integration path rather than trust in an LLM. The heuristic encoded the phase boundary directly. The recorded Q-learning behaviour is consistent with an insufficient response under this formulation, but does not identify its cause.

\paragraph{Relation to literature.} The validation boundary responds to reliability and security concerns in LLM-assisted networking \citep{lira2025llmnetcfg,liu2025llmnetworking}. Digital-twin-assisted RL can improve learning in other designs \citep{guemes2022accelerating,cheng2025dtrl}, but those results do not guarantee superiority in this low-dimensional ordered task.

\paragraph{Limitation.} One compatible-provider record is not official OpenAI success. Q-learning rows 1--4 were migrated observations, while rows 5--20 used a reinitialized agent without restoration or replay of the earlier Q-table. The 20 valid measurement rows therefore do not form an uninterrupted learning trajectory. One seed, fixed hyperparameters, and this ordered formulation cannot show whether another duration or formulation would switch paths; rows are not independent randomized replications. The Dashboard is only a supporting interface.

\subsection{Threats to validity and limitations}

\paragraph{Internal validity.} Experiments share a host, Docker engine, kernel, and virtual networking stack. Uncontrolled host load and scheduling can influence throughput and timing. The retained runners, route/qdisc checks, and repeated cohorts reduce but do not remove this risk. The balanced bandwidth cohort completed all 15 planned runs without retry, but its fixed block order does not separate rate from session time.

\paragraph{Construct validity.} Configured one-way delay and loss are not the same constructs as measured RTT and end-to-end ping loss. TCP throughput is affected by more than the configured TBF rate. Reward is specific to the retained RL formulation and should not be generalized to all network objectives.

\paragraph{Statistical conclusion validity.} Delay and loss have overall rank-based permutation tests, but $n=5$ per group limits precision and no pairwise inference is claimed. Bandwidth intervals are descriptive and its blocks are ordered; Germany50 is confounded; RL measurement rows are ordered and dependent; Qwen and Dashboard are single records. These designs do not support additional significance claims.

\paragraph{External validity.} Results apply to the evaluated Docker/WSL environment, topologies, traffic tools, and impairment ranges. Equivalence with hardware routers, production workloads, other kernels, or wide-area networks was not tested. Germany50 represents structural scale but only three real traffic paths. The Q-learning result applies to one state/action/reward design and one hyperparameter setting.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and evidence preservation.} Scenarios, raw metrics, summaries, scripts, plots, failures, and cleanup records provide substantial provenance. Some statistics in this draft are AC derivatives and remain separate from repository evidence. Later reviewer-readiness checks establish a practical fresh-clone path and 69 passing unit tests, but they post-date the formal experiments and do not increase their sample sizes or coverage. Raw historical Dashboard smoke logs remain unavailable.

The main RQ has a qualified positive answer within the tested scope. One validated pipeline enforced and measured controlled conditions, then supported multi-router, Germany50 structural, guarded-generation, and measured-control cases. In practice, it provides a reproducible and auditable testbed for controlled container-network experiments before hardware or operational testing, but it does not establish production behaviour or physical-network fidelity. Its evidence does not establish exhaustive topology traffic, provider reliability, or learning-policy superiority. Traceability supports auditing through linked inputs, checks, measurements, and artifacts; it is not treated as a measured outcome.
