\begin{abstract}
Network digital twins (NDTs) promise safer, repeatable network experimentation, but evidence is often divided among architecture proposals, emulation studies, topology evaluations, and automated-control demonstrations. This study evaluates a scenario-driven, reproducibility-oriented Docker NDT using validated scenarios, deterministic routing, Linux traffic control, ping and iperf3 measurements, and structured evidence retention. The evaluation covers controlled bandwidth, one-way delay and loss, direct and multi-router topologies, Germany50 scaling, artificial-intelligence (AI)-assisted scenario generation, and reinforcement-learning (RL) control. Fifteen valid fixed-block bandwidth runs, five each at configured rates of 20, 50, and 100 Mbps, produced mean throughputs of 19.20, 47.80, and 94.76 Mbps. Configured one-way delays of 10, 30, and 50 ms produced mean round-trip times (RTTs) of 27.008, 80.303, and 133.686 ms across five runs per condition. The complete 50-node/88-link Germany50 topology was instantiated, but its 4,224-entry route plan was validated only in dry-run mode and real Docker traffic covered three representative paths, not all pairs. The official OpenAI request returned HTTP 429, whereas a separate Qwen-compatible request passed validation and controlled execution. Across 80 valid real-Docker measurements from four control policies, the threshold heuristic achieved a higher mean reward than the evaluated Q-learning configuration. The results support an artifact-backed experimental workflow in the tested configurations while exposing limits in host fidelity, topology coverage, provider availability, evidence preservation, and learning-based control performance.
\end{abstract}
